# Background

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) is a Eurasian coordination platform whose members use summits and working arrangements to pursue regional security, diplomatic and economic cooperation. It is not a collective-defence treaty alliance: participation does not create an automatic obligation to fight for another member. This distinction is central to interpreting its value to [China]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/china.html), [Russia]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/russia.html), and states in [Central Asia]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/central-asia.html).

# Current Situation

The Bishkek summit scheduled for 31 August 2026 placed the SCO alongside bilateral meetings between Vladimir Putin and leaders including Xi Jinping, Narendra Modi, Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and Masoud Pezeshkian. The reported agenda illustrates the forum's convening role rather than evidence of an integrated military command. [1]

# Analysis

## Platform Rather Than Alliance

[Velina Tchakarova]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/authors/velina-tchakarova.html) reads the SCO as one of the institutional platforms through which China and Russia can coordinate a multipolar agenda while retaining strategic autonomy and bilateral bargaining space. The Bishkek meeting is therefore best understood as a venue for the reported bilateral agenda, not proof of an integrated bloc. [1] That usage is compatible with her DragonBear framework, which describes coordination rather than a formal alliance. [2]
