# Background

Gerald Willmann is a trade theorist at Bielefeld University with a long-standing affiliation with the Kiel Institute for the World Economy. His research spans international trade theory, quantitative trade models, and the economics of regional integration. He is a co-author of Kiel Policy Brief No. 206, where his theoretical work underpins the bottleneck mechanism that extends the KITE model beyond standard Cobb-Douglas substitution assumptions. See [Kiel Institute](kiel-institute.html) for the institutional context.

# Key Frameworks

## Bottleneck Mechanism Derivation

Willmann's contribution to KPB 206 is the theoretical derivation of the bottleneck mechanism that extends the Caliendo & Parro (2015) framework with a critical-input penalty. The core formula introduces a one-sided penalty term — using a max(1, ·) operator — that applies only when critical inputs become more expensive, not when they become cheaper. This asymmetry captures the real-world observation that bottleneck costs bite hard on the upside but generate no symmetric windfall on the downside. The penalty is weighted by critical-input exposure, normalized to capture each sector's dependence on bottleneck inputs such as natural gas for fertilizer production [1].
