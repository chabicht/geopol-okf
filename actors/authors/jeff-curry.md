# Background

Jeff Currie is a commodity and energy market analyst who built his career at Goldman Sachs, where he served as global head of commodities research for over two decades. He subsequently joined Bank of America. His expertise spans oil supply-demand dynamics, refining capacity, crack spreads, and the interaction between geopolitical disruptions and commodity price transmission. In the bundle, he appears as the energy-specialist collaborator in [Robert Pape](robert-pape.html)'s August 2026 live briefing on the [US-Iran War]({{ site.baseurl }}/conflicts/us-iran-war-2026.html), where his commodity-market analysis complements Pape's escalation-trap framework.

# Key Frameworks

## Crude-Refining Double Bottleneck

Currie's central contribution to the August 2026 briefing is the identification of a double bottleneck in global energy markets: the crude-supply shock from the Hormuz closure is compounded by a parallel collapse in refining capacity, driven in part by Ukraine's strikes on Russian refineries. The relevant consumer product is gasoline and diesel, not crude itself; the crack spread — the margin between buying crude and selling refined products — reached $70 per barrel, the highest ever recorded. This framework explains why higher gasoline prices arrive even before crude buffers are fully exhausted, as refining constraints add a second layer of price pressure on top of the crude shortage.

## Refining Capacity Asymmetry Between China and the US

Currie identifies a structural asymmetry: China maintains a substantial refining surplus while the US is running at or above capacity. Over time, China can offer refining services and set geopolitical terms, positioning Beijing to benefit from the crisis regardless of how the Hormuz situation resolves. This insight connects the energy-market analysis to broader [Great Power Competition]({{ site.baseurl }}/themes/great-power-competition.html) dynamics.
