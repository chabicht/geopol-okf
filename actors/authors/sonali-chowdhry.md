# Background

Sonali Chowdhry is an economist affiliated with DIW Berlin (the German Institute for Economic Research) and the Kiel Institute for the World Economy. Her research focuses on international trade, export competition, and the economic impacts of China's export expansion on advanced economies. She is the lead author of Kiel Report No. 213, which decomposes advanced-economy market-share losses in third markets into China-attributable and home-grown components. See [Kiel Institute](kiel-institute.html) for the institutional context.

# Key Frameworks

## Proportional Benchmark and Excess-Loss Decomposition

Chowdhry's central methodological contribution in KPB 213 is the "proportional benchmark" — a counterfactual that measures the market-share loss each advanced economy should experience purely as a mechanical consequence of China's growing share of a third market, assuming all other competitive forces remain unchanged. By comparing actual market-share changes to this benchmark, the methodology decomposes each country's losses into the share mechanically attributable to China's rise and an "excess loss" share reflecting domestic competitiveness deterioration. The finding that 80.2% of German sectors and 89.6% of UK sectors lose disproportionately — far exceeding what China's expansion alone would predict — is a direct output of this framework. The policy implication is that blanket tariffs are poorly matched to the problem, since a large share of the losses is home-grown rather than China-driven [1].
