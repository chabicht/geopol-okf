# Background

Hendrik Mahlkow is an economist affiliated with Kiel University and the Kiel Institute for the World Economy. His research focuses on macroeconomic modeling and quantitative trade analysis. He is a co-developer of the KITE bottleneck extension and co-author of Kiel Policy Brief No. 206, which simulates the economic consequences of a full closure of the Strait of Hormuz. See [Kiel Institute](kiel-institute.html) for the institutional context.

# Key Frameworks

## KITE Bottleneck Extension

Mahlkow co-developed the bottleneck extension to the KITE quantitative general equilibrium model alongside [Julian Hinz](julian-hinz.html) and [Robin Sogalla](robin-sogalla.html). The extension introduces a critical-input penalty that captures asymmetric cost transmission through bottleneck inputs — such as natural gas as feedstock for fertilizer production. The one-sided penalty mechanism ensures that chokepoint costs bite on the upside but do not generate symmetric gains on the downside, correcting a systematic underestimation in standard trade models. The model's short-run scenarios use elasticities set to one-quarter of long-run values, reflecting the severely limited substitution possibilities in the immediate aftermath of a disruption [1].
