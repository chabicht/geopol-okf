# Background

Katharina Erhardt is an economist affiliated with Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf and the Kiel Institute for the World Economy. Her research centers on international trade and the competitive dynamics of China's export expansion. She is a co-author of Kiel Report No. 213, which uses BACI/CEPII trade data to decompose advanced-economy market-share losses in third markets into China-attributable and home-grown components. See [Kiel Institute](kiel-institute.html) for the institutional context.

# Key Frameworks

## Third-Market Competition Analysis

Erhardt's contribution to KPB 213 centers on the empirical analysis of how China's export expansion has displaced advanced-economy exporters in third markets, using a ten-year comparison window (2012–2014 vs 2022–2024). The analysis covers the EU+7 bloc — all EU member states plus Norway, Switzerland, the UK, the US, Canada, Japan, and South Korea — and finds that China's gains are concentrated in complex manufacturing sectors where advanced economies lost the most. The cross-country heterogeneity is a key finding: Germany, Japan, France, and the UK suffered large excess losses, while South Korea, Switzerland, and the US maintained or improved their position relative to other non-Chinese exporters [1].
