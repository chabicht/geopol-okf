# Background

Algeria is an energy-exporting state under President Abdelmadjid Tebboune. Algeria provides ~18% of EU gas imports via the Transmed pipeline (to Italy) and Medgaz pipeline (to Spain). The country uses more than half of its gas domestically, limiting export growth. New production depends on foreign investment (ExxonMobil, Chevron) and controversial shale-gas extraction with environmental and protest risks.

# Current Situation

The Hormuz crisis has elevated Algeria's strategic importance as Europe seeks alternatives to Gulf energy. Pipeline infrastructure makes Algeria a cornerstone of European energy security. However, infrastructure faces modernization limits, and shale-gas expansion carries environmental and social risks. Algeria was the EU's second-largest gas supplier at 17.4% of total imports in 2025. # Key Dynamics

- **EU gas supplier anchor.** Algeria provides ~18% of EU gas imports via the Transmed and Medgaz pipelines, making it the EU's second-largest gas supplier at 17.4% of total imports in 2025. [1]
- **Export growth constrained by domestic consumption.** Algeria uses more than half of its gas domestically, limiting export growth despite elevated European demand for Gulf alternatives. [1]
- **Shale-gas expansion carries social risk.** New production depends on foreign investment and controversial shale-gas extraction, which carries environmental and protest risks. [1]
- **Infrastructure modernization bottleneck.** Pipeline infrastructure faces modernization limits that constrain Algeria's ability to fully capitalize on its elevated strategic energy position. [1]
