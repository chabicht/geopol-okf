# Background

Norway is a high-income European economy whose export base combines energy commodities (oil, gas) with advanced manufacturing and seafood. As a member of the "EU+7" bloc analyzed in KPB 213 (EU plus Norway, Switzerland, UK, US, Canada, Japan, South Korea), Norway is grouped among the advanced economies facing [China]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/china.html)'s export competition in third markets. Norway's energy endowment gives it a distinctive export profile within this bloc. See [China Shock and Deindustrialization]({{ site.baseurl }}/themes/china-shock-and-deindustrialization.html).

# Current Situation

## Third-Market Competition with China

KPB 213 identifies Norway as the clearest "winner" in the dataset — the only economy with a positive weighted market-share change (+1.32 percentage points), meaning Norwegian firms actually gained overall market share in third markets over the 2012–2024 period. Norway's excess-loss rate of 63.5% indicates that roughly 36% of its losses (where they occurred) are mechanically attributable to China's rise, but the net effect was positive. This performance stands in sharp contrast to [Germany]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/germany.html) (−1.98pp), [Japan]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/japan.html) (−1.97pp), and [France]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/france.html) (−1.53pp) [1].

# Key Dynamics

- **Norway is the clearest "winner" — the only economy with a positive weighted Δ.** At +1.32pp, Norway actually gained market share in third markets while peers lost ground. [1]
- **Energy endowment differentiates Norway's export profile.** Its oil and gas base anchors an export basket distinct from the manufacturing-heavy profiles of Germany, Japan, and South Korea. [1]
- **Moderate excess-loss rate but net-positive outcome.** At 63.5%, Norway's excess-loss rate is middling, but the aggregate effect is favorable because gains outweigh losses. [1]
- **The EU+7 framing places Norway in the advanced-economy bloc.** KPB 213 groups Norway with the EU, US, Japan, and others as economies facing Chinese competition in third markets. [1]
