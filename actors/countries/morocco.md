# Background

Morocco is a constitutional monarchy under King Mohammed VI, strategically located at the Strait of Gibraltar. Tanger Med is Africa's leading container port. Morocco has FTAs with 50+ countries, making it a manufacturing hub for Chinese firms seeking tariff-free EU access. Phosphate reserves are among the world's largest. Renewable energy ambition: 17% of production from renewables, but still imports ~90% of energy demand. Hydrogen program underway.

# Current Situation

The Hormuz crisis has accelerated Morocco's emergence as a trade and energy hub. Chinese-partnered battery factories and green-energy production position Morocco as a beneficiary of European supply-chain diversification. Compensation spending (~1.2% of GDP) masks underlying inflation vulnerability. The country remains vulnerable to energy-price shocks despite renewable ambitions. # Key Dynamics

- **Trade-hub positioning.** Tanger Med is Africa's leading container port, with FTA access to 50+ countries making Morocco a manufacturing hub for Chinese firms seeking tariff-free EU access. [1]
- **Energy import vulnerability.** Morocco imports ~90% of its energy demand despite 17% renewable production, leaving it acutely exposed to Hormuz-driven energy-price shocks. [1]
- **Phosphate and battery strategic reserves.** Morocco's phosphate reserves are among the world's largest, and Chinese-partnered battery factories position it as a beneficiary of European supply-chain diversification. [1]
- **Compensation spending masks inflation risk.** Compensation spending at ~1.2% of GDP shields households from price shocks but conceals underlying inflation vulnerability. [1]
