# Background

Morocco is a constitutional monarchy under King Mohammed VI, strategically located just 14 kilometers from Europe across the Strait of Gibraltar. Tanger Med is Africa's leading container port. Morocco has FTAs with 50+ countries, making it a manufacturing hub for Chinese firms seeking tariff-free EU access. Phosphate reserves are among the world's largest. Renewable energy ambition: 17% of production from renewables, but still imports ~90% of energy demand. Hydrogen program underway.

# Current Situation

The Hormuz crisis has accelerated Morocco's emergence as a trade and energy hub. Chinese-partnered battery factories and green-energy production position Morocco as a beneficiary of European supply-chain diversification. Compensation spending (~1.2% of GDP) masks underlying inflation vulnerability. The country remains vulnerable to energy-price shocks despite renewable ambitions. GPF (GeoEconomicLens August 2026) provides a comprehensive structural profile of Morocco as an emerging geostrategic hub. The country combines political and macroeconomic stability, a growing industrial base, and comparatively strong infrastructure with persistent regional disparities, high youth unemployment, water scarcity, and uneven household purchasing power. Its geographic position — close to Europe, connected to Atlantic and Mediterranean shipping routes, integrated into African supply networks, and supported by preferential Western market access — is becoming a central investment consideration as global trade corridors reconfigure [2].

## Western Sahara and Territorial Leverage

The Western Sahara dispute remains a defining feature of Moroccan foreign policy. Morocco controls roughly 80% of the territory and has converted the dispute into an instrument of diplomatic leverage. The US recognized Moroccan sovereignty in December 2020, and the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 2797 in October 2025, calling for negotiations based on Morocco's 2007 autonomy plan. Spain and France have lent support. However, the territory's international legal status remains unresolved: the EU Court of Justice held in October 2024 that EU-Morocco trade arrangements cannot extend to Western Sahara without its people's consent, forcing renegotiations. The Polisario Front continues to reject the autonomy framework and has initiated new legal challenges [2].

## Commercial Profile and International Ties

EU-Morocco bilateral trade exceeded €62 billion in 2025, with Spain as Morocco's principal individual trading partner and primary physical gateway to Europe. France is a major investor, particularly in automotive, aerospace, and financial services. China has rapidly become Morocco's third-largest trading partner ($10.96 billion in 2025) but the relationship is highly asymmetric: Chinese exports totaled $9.88 billion versus Moroccan exports of only $1.08 billion. Morocco's participation in the Belt and Road Initiative since 2017 has shifted the relationship toward industrial investment — the centerpiece is Gotion High-Tech's gigafactory near Kenitra ($1.3 billion initial investment, 20 GWh initial capacity scaling to 100 GWh), and Stellantis's €1.2 billion Kenitra expansion [2].

Moroccan corporate reach extends across Africa: Attijariwafa Bank operates in 27 countries, Bank of Africa in 20, Maroc Telecom across 11 countries serving 78 million customers, and OCP Africa is active in 42 countries. Casablanca hosts the Africa50 pan-African infrastructure platform and markets itself as a base for managing continental operations [2].

## Infrastructure and the Atlantic Initiative

King Mohammed VI's November 2023 Atlantic Initiative positions Morocco as the international gateway for landlocked Sahel states, giving them Atlantic trade access via roads and rail connected to the $1.2 billion Dakhla deep-water port (expected completion 2028, 1,650-hectare industrial zone, 35 million metric tons/year capacity) — challenging Algeria's position as the main channel for Sahelian states to the Mediterranean [2].

Tanger Med, Morocco's crown jewel, handled 161 million metric tons of cargo in 2025 and ranks sixth globally in the World Bank's port efficiency index. Nador West Med ($5.6 billion, 5 million containers initial capacity, Morocco's first LNG terminal) is under construction. Jorf Lasfar is the world's leading phosphate and fertilizer-exporting port [2].

## Phosphates, Renewables, and Energy Infrastructure

OCP Group manages an estimated 68% of world phosphate reserves and holds roughly 31% of the global phosphate products market. OCP is investing $5 billion to expand output from 12 million to 20 million tons by 2027 and $13 billion by 2032 in solar and wind power for green ammonia self-sufficiency. Morocco's renewables target is 52% of installed capacity by 2030; the "Morocco Offer" program encompasses 20 GW of renewable energy, 10 GW of electrolysis, and 8 million tons of green hydrogen derivatives — though some projects sit in Sahrawi-claimed provinces, raising legal concerns [2].

A proposed $25 billion Nigeria-Morocco gas pipeline backed by ECOWAS heads of state in July 2026 would carry up to 30 billion cubic meters of Nigerian gas through West African coastal waters toward Morocco and into Europe. Automotive manufacturing has become Morocco's largest export earner, making it Africa's largest car exporter by volume and the EU's leading non-member vehicle supplier [2].

## OCP Trade Dispute and Domestic Unrest

The Trump administration temporarily suspended countervailing duties on Moroccan OCP phosphate fertilizers on June 29, 2026, for up to eight months amid global supply disruptions and insufficient US production. The decision does not permanently resolve the dispute but demonstrates Morocco's growing value as a dependable source of agricultural inputs. Domestically, the GenZ212 movement organized protests in September-October 2025 demanding better healthcare, education, employment, and government accountability — the most unruly bout of unrest since the Arab Spring, prompting increased health and education spending for 2026 [2].

# Key Dynamics

- **Trade-hub positioning.** Tanger Med handled 161M mt cargo in 2025, ranked 6th globally by the World Bank; Nador West Med ($5.6B) and Dakhla port ($1.2B, 2028) are expanding Morocco's Atlantic-Mediterranean gateway role. [1][2]
- **Western Sahara as diplomatic leverage.** Morocco controls ~80% of the territory; US recognition (2020), UNSC Res 2797 (2025), and EU renegotiations all strengthen Rabat's position while legal status remains unresolved. [2]
- **OCP's phosphate dominance.** Morocco holds 68% of world phosphate reserves and ~31% market share, investing $5B to expand to 20M tons by 2027 and $13B in green ammonia by 2032. [2]
- **China-Morocco industrial axis.** Gotion's $1.3B Kenitra gigafactory (20→100 GWh), Stellantis €1.2B expansion, and $10.96B trade (asymmetric) position Morocco as China's European-adjacent manufacturing platform. [2]
- **Atlantic Initiative challenges Algeria.** The Dakhla port gives landlocked Sahel states an Atlantic alternative to Algeria's Mediterranean corridor, reshaping regional competition. [2]
- **Moroccan corporate reach across Africa.** Attijariwafa (27 countries), Bank of Africa (20), Maroc Telecom (11/78M customers), OCP Africa (42 countries) give Morocco continental influence. [2]
- **Energy import vulnerability persists.** Morocco imports ~90% of its energy demand despite 17% renewable production and 52% by 2030 targets, leaving it acutely exposed to energy-price shocks. [1]
- **GenZ212 unrest signals social pressure.** Sept-Oct 2025 protests were the worst since the Arab Spring, prompting increased social spending but underlying youth unemployment and regional disparities remain. [2]
