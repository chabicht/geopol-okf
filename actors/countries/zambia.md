# Background

Zambia is a landlocked Southern African economy with high dependence on imported fertilizers and food, limited domestic energy production, and thin fiscal buffers. These structural features make it acutely vulnerable to disruptions in global energy and agricultural supply chains, particularly those routed through the Strait of Hormuz. See [Food and Agriculture]({{ site.baseurl }}/themes/food-and-agriculture.html) and [Energy and Resources]({{ site.baseurl }}/themes/energy-and-resources.html).

# Current Situation

## Hormuz Closure Vulnerability

KPB 206's KITE bottleneck model simulation of a full Strait of Hormuz closure identifies Zambia as the most extreme data point among all countries studied. Zambia faces a −5.49% welfare loss — roughly 78 times the US figure (−0.07%) — and a +30.7% food price increase, the highest in the dataset. This asymmetry illustrates how energy-to-food cascades transmitted through fertilizer supply chains hit Global South economies with the least capacity to absorb them. The simulation captures only structural trade-channel effects; real-world outcomes would include additional speculative and hoarding dynamics [1].

# Key Dynamics

- **Zambia is the most extreme case in the Hormuz closure simulation.** A −5.49% welfare loss and +30.7% food price increase make it the worst-affected country in KPB 206's dataset. [1]
- **Fertilizer dependence drives the food-price cascade.** Zambia's reliance on imported fertilizers — much of it sourced from Gulf Haber-Bosch production — transmits energy shocks directly into food prices. [1]
- **Asymmetric vulnerability defines Global South exposure.** Zambia's losses are 10–20 times larger than the US in percentage terms, hitting populations with far less fiscal capacity to absorb them. [1]
- **Structural features compound the shock.** Landlocked geography, limited domestic energy production, and thin fiscal buffers leave Zambia with minimal substitution options in the short run. [1]
- **The model captures structural costs, not spot prices.** The simulation excludes speculative dynamics, hoarding, and reserve releases — real-world impacts would likely exceed the modeled figures. [1]
