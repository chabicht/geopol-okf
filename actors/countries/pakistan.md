# Background

Pakistan is a nuclear-armed state whose strategic position bridges the [Middle East]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/middle-east.html), [Central Asia]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/central-asia.html), and the [Indo-Pacific]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/indo-pacific.html). Its relationships with [Saudi Arabia]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/saudi-arabia.html), [China]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/china.html), and the [United States]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/united-states.html) are structurally entangled: Pakistan depends on Saudi financial support, Chinese military and infrastructure investment, and periodic US engagement. The [US-Iran War of 2026]({{ site.baseurl }}/conflicts/us-iran-war-2026.html) elevated Pakistan's role as a regional mediator and security provider.

# Current Situation

## Saudi-Iran Mediation

Pakistan is mediating secret negotiations between [Saudi Arabia]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/saudi-arabia.html) and [Iran]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/iran.html), with Russian and Chinese support. The talks aim at a potential non-aggression pact that would stabilize the Gulf without direct US involvement. Pakistan's credibility with both Riyadh and Tehran — rooted in religious, geographic, and historical ties — makes it the only actor that can facilitate this channel [1].

## Troop Deployment to Saudi Arabia

Pakistan has deployed troops to Saudi Arabia as part of a military buildup coinciding with the Iran war. The deployment signals Pakistan's alignment with the Saudi security architecture while maintaining its diplomatic role as mediator. Chinese jets have also been deployed to Saudi Arabia, creating a trilateral security relationship that bypasses the US framework [1].

## Mecca Joint Defense Agreement

[Robert Pape]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/authors/robert-pape.html) identifies Pakistan as a participant in the Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan "Mecca" defense agreement. Pape assesses the pact as a power-vacuum fill rather than a new NATO: it lacks an integrated command structure, and Turkey and Saudi Arabia are unlikely to hand over control of their forces to a Pakistani general. The pact fills the security vacuum left by declining American power, but its collective-defense commitment is unlikely to be tested by a Pakistan-[India]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/india.html) nuclear crisis [3].

## Hormuz Closure Food-Price Vulnerability

The Kiel Institute's KITE bottleneck simulation identifies Pakistan among the most food-price-vulnerable economies under a full Hormuz closure, with a projected +11.40% food price increase and −1.83% welfare loss in the short-run scenario — reflecting Pakistan's dependence on fertilizer and food imports transiting through the chokepoint [4].

- **Troops signal alignment with Saudi security architecture.** Pakistan has deployed troops to Saudi Arabia as part of a military buildup coinciding with the Iran war, while maintaining its mediation role. [1]
- **Trilateral security bypasses the US.** Chinese jets deployed alongside Pakistani troops to Saudi Arabia create a Pakistan-China-Saudi security relationship operating outside the US framework. [1]
- **A non-aggression pact would stabilize the Gulf without Washington.** The talks, backed by Russia and China, aim to reduce Gulf tensions without direct US involvement. [1]
- **Pakistan is part of the "core" of a new post-Iran-War regional security architecture.** Alongside Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Egypt, Pakistan would take a leadership role as Washington transitions to offshore balancer. [2]

# Analysis

## Leadership Role in Post-Iran-War Architecture

[Kamran Bokhari]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/authors/kamran-bokhari.html) (GPF) identifies Pakistan as part of the "core" of a new regional security architecture emerging in the Middle East, alongside [Turkey]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/turkey.html), [Saudi Arabia]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/saudi-arabia.html), and [Egypt]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/egypt.html) — with support from Qatar, the UAE, Jordan, and Azerbaijan. As Washington transitions from regional hegemon to offshore actor, these countries would take a leadership role to create a self-sustaining balance of power. The challenge is not only aligning these countries — all of which have their own interests and priorities — but integrating them into a workable relationship with [Israel]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/israel.html). Pakistan lacks diplomatic ties with Israel, complicating this integration [2]. See [Middle East]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/middle-east.html) and [US-Iran War 2026]({{ site.baseurl }}/conflicts/us-iran-war-2026.html).

Bokhari notes that the same Iranian capabilities that animate US and Israeli fears — the proxy network, ballistic missiles and drones, and the pursuit of nuclear weapons — also animate Pakistan's strategic concerns. For the Arab states, especially those on the Arabian Peninsula, the growth of Iranian offensive military capabilities is a strategic vulnerability. Iran attacking Arab states as and when it has an interest in doing so has become the new normal, shaping regional calculations for years to come [2]. See [Iran]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/iran.html).
