# Background

South Korea is a major advanced manufacturing economy whose export portfolio spans vehicles, electronics, machinery, and chemicals — sectors where [China]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/china.html)'s export expansion has been most aggressive. As a member of the "EU+7" bloc (EU plus Norway, Switzerland, UK, US, Canada, Japan, South Korea) analyzed in KPB 213, South Korea occupies a distinctive position: it competes directly with China in complex manufacturing but has adapted more successfully than peers such as [Germany]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/germany.html), [Japan]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/japan.html), and the [United Kingdom]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/united-kingdom.html). See [China Shock and Deindustrialization]({{ site.baseurl }}/themes/china-shock-and-deindustrialization.html).

# Current Situation

## Third-Market Competition with China

KPB 213's proportional-benchmark analysis finds that South Korea is a "winner" in the non-Chinese market segment — its firms gained competitiveness relative to other non-Chinese exporters in third markets over the 2012–2024 period, despite an overall weighted market-share change of −0.37 percentage points. South Korea's excess-loss rate of 49% is among the lowest in the dataset, meaning roughly half of its market-share losses are mechanically attributable to China's rise rather than to home-grown competitiveness deterioration — a far better ratio than Germany (80.2%) or the UK (89.6%) [1].

# Key Dynamics

- **South Korea is a "winner" in non-Chinese third-market competition.** Its firms gained market share relative to other non-Chinese exporters despite overall losses, adapting more successfully than Germany, Japan, or the UK. [1]
- **Low excess-loss rate signals resilience.** At 49%, South Korea's excess-loss rate means only about half of its losses exceed what China's rise alone would predict — far below Germany (80.2%) or the UK (89.6%). [1]
- **Complex manufacturing is the competitive frontier.** South Korea competes directly with China in vehicles, electronics, and machinery — sectors where China's export share has grown most — yet has maintained relative position. [1]
- **The EU+7 framing situates South Korea in the advanced-economy bloc.** The KPB 213 definition groups South Korea with the EU, US, Japan, and others as the bloc losing market share where China gains. [1]
