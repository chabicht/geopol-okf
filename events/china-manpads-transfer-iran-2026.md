# Background

Through the first six months of the [US-Iran War]({{ site.baseurl }}/conflicts/us-iran-war-2026.html), Chinese support for [Iran]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/iran.html) consisted of dual-use components and intelligence sharing: missile propellant chemicals (sodium perchlorate shipped via IRISL vessels from Zhuhai, with at least five shipments tracked — enough for ~785 additional missiles), satellite intelligence (the TEE-01B spy satellite monitoring US bases across Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, Djibouti, UAE, and Iraq), AI-enabled satellite analysis marketed by Chinese firms, BeiDou navigation data for targeting, and chipmaking equipment supplied by SMIC. An F-15E downed in southwestern Iran in April was likely hit by a Chinese-made shoulder-launched missile [1].

# What Happened

On July 29, 2026, Reuters reported that [China]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/china.html) was preparing to send Iran 400 advanced MANPADS launchers — FN-16 and QW-12 systems, the latest generation of Chinese man-portable air defense, comparable to the US Stinger. First shipment was expected within weeks [1].

# Analysis

[Phillips O'Brien]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/authors/phillips-obrien.html) frames this as a qualitative escalation — the transfer of finished advanced weapons rather than dual-use components. MANPADS are ideally suited to thwart US escalation because ground operations (supply runs, evacuation, patrols) require low-altitude flights near Kharg Island or inside Iran. The weapons would create a regular succession of vulnerable targets, likely escalating already historic US airpower losses. Trump faces a binary choice: escalate before they arrive (risky) or admit he cannot escalate at all. The weapons are small, easy to operate, easy to ship — and could deploy by September [1].

Even as Cabinet members admitted to Congress that China and Russia were enabling Iran's war effort, [Trump]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/trump.html) posted on Truth Social (July 24) that Xi had told him he would not "partake" and that Trump trusted him: "I think you know I trust them… I don't think they'd want to have me disappointed." O'Brien compares the moment to 1861, when the US was weak enough for external powers to directly threaten it — except unlike then, the US is not on an upward trajectory [1].
