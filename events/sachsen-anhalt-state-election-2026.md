# Background

The September 20, 2026 Saxony-Anhalt state election carries national and alliance-level significance far beyond a regional vote. INSA polling places the AfD at 41% versus CDU at 24% — putting the AfD within reach of an absolute majority and sole control of a state government for the first time. The AfD is classified as "gesichert rechtsextrem" (confirmed right-extremist) by the Verfassungsschutz.

# Stakes

An AfD sole government would control the state Interior Ministry, state police (Landespolizei), state domestic intelligence (Landesverfassungsschutz), and much of the state administration. This has direct NATO implications: Germany's role as NATO's logistical hub depends on Länder-level cooperation for troop movements, police escorts, fuel, customs, and permits during reinforcement operations.

# The NATO Logistics Threat

The German federal government, across multiple ministries (Interior, Justice, Defense), is drafting emergency plans ("Notfallpläne") to safeguard NATO troop deployment logistics if an AfD-led Land obstructs operations. The plans include: bypassing the Land government for intelligence-sharing (Pistorius: "we are intensively examining to whom we can grant access to classified information"); neighbouring-Land takeover of critical-infrastructure protection; and constitutional mechanisms including Article 37 Bundeszwang (federal coercion, never invoked in FRG history). The Bundestag two-thirds emergency majority is blocked because Merz has ruled out cooperation with both Linke and AfD.

# AfD Positioning

AfD Spitzenkandidat Ulrich Siegmund explicitly advocates lifting Russia sanctions and importing Russian gas: "We will of course put the topic of ideological energy policy on the agenda — and in such a way that we advocate strongly against sanctions, for cheap energy, no matter which country." When asked if "from Russia" is included: "Yes, of course."

# Analysis

Kiesewetter (CDU, Auswärtiger Ausschuss) argues the Kremlin is "exploiting divergent Russian-policy attitudes within Germany" to make the logistics hub "as unreliable as possible." A western defense source frames Russia's strategy: "the fastest way to break the Alliance is to find and deepen political fault lines." An AfD Land government could "slow" but not "stop" the Operationsplan Deutschland — main delay points: state police manpower, transmission of directives, execution of operational plans.
