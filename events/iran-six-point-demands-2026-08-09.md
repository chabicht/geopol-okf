# Background

The June 17 [ceasefire MoU]({{ site.baseurl }}/events/iran-ceasefire-mou.html) established a post-paid concession structure: Hormuz would reopen, then concessions would be negotiated. By August 2026, that structure had collapsed. [Iran]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/iran.html)'s Supreme National Security Council moved to replace it with pre-conditions — demands that must be met before Hormuz reopens at all. This shift from post-paid to pre-paid marks a transition from a bargaining framework to what [Robert Pape]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/authors/robert-pape.html) terms "victor's-justice" terms: the posture of a state that believes it won the war and is dictating conditions, not negotiating. [1]

# What Happened

Iran's Supreme National Security Council issued a six-point set of demands that must be satisfied before the [Strait of Hormuz]({{ site.baseurl }}/events/hormuz-toll-system.html) reopens. The demands as reported include:

- Approximately $300 billion in up-front payment for damages.
- Over $100 billion in frozen Iranian assets returned.
- An end to Israel's occupation of Palestine — not a negotiation, but an end.
- Removal of US military forces from the Middle East.
- Additional conditions on sanctions relief and diplomatic recognition.

The scale is in the hundreds of billions of dollars. The New York Times reported the demands as dragging the US back to the MoU framework; Pape disagrees, reading them as victor's-justice terms — akin to what the Allies imposed on Germany after WWII, without negotiating with the defeated power. [1]

# Analysis

Pape frames the six-point demands as the diplomatic manifestation of Iran's transition from survival to ambition. The demands represent a fundamental change in negotiation posture: the MoU was "let's get together and talk, we'll open Hormuz, then we'll talk and get concessions." The new demands require "everything up front" before Hormuz reopens. This is not a return to bargaining — it is the posture of a state that reads itself as having won the [US-Iran War]({{ site.baseurl }}/conflicts/us-iran-war-2026.html). [1]

Pape characterizes Iran's approach as "balance-of-power politics played by a black belt" against a US "behaving like green belts." The underestimation of Iran is, in his view, a primary reason US strategy has failed to produce the desired outcomes. Iran's period of maximum leverage runs through January 2027 — the midterm clock — and Tehran's objective is to "drive a stake through the heart of the Trump presidency" via economic pressure rather than to reach a negotiated settlement. [1]

The demands connect to Pape's Stage Four of the [Escalation Theory Critique]({{ site.baseurl }}/themes/escalation-theory-critique.html) — strategic paralysis. If the US continues inaction, Iran becomes the rising regional hegemon in the Persian Gulf, the fourth center of world power. Under that scenario, Pape forecasts that Iran would escalate beyond Hormuz to topple MBS and organize assassination attempts against Gulf monarchies. The six-point demands are the diplomatic leading edge of that trajectory. [1]

A key analytical tension: punishment strategy — the assumption that inflicting costs will produce compliance — produces fight, not flight. Pape's *Bombing to Win* finding, generalized to the current context, holds that killing 4,000 Iranians makes them want to hurt the US, not surrender. The six-point demands are consistent with this: a punished Iran is not a compliant Iran, but an Iran that reads its survival as vindication and presses its advantage. [1]

## August 11 Refinement: Victor's-Justice Terms and the Collision Course

[Robert Pape]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/authors/robert-pape.html)'s August 11 analysis restates the demands with precise dollar figures: approximately $300 billion in up-front payment for damages, over $100 billion in frozen Iranian assets returned, an end to Israel's occupation of Palestine, removal of US military forces from the Middle East, sanctions relief, and diplomatic recognition. The scale and simultaneity of these demands confirm Pape's characterization as "victor's-justice terms" — the posture of a state dictating conditions to a defeated adversary, not bargaining with one. The demands are consistent with the Survival→Ambition→Hard Power progression: Iran's survival through the US bombing campaign vindicated its strategic position, the six-point demands operationalized that vindication into ambition, and the subsequent [collision with Trump's own unilateral demands]({{ site.baseurl }}/events/hormuz-unilateral-demands-2026-08-11.html) converted ambition into a hard-power standoff where neither side can accept the other's terms [2].
