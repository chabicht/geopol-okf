# Background

The South Caucasus comprises [Azerbaijan]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/azerbaijan.html), Armenia, and Georgia — three states squeezed between [Russia]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/russia.html) to the north, [Iran]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/iran.html) to the south, and [Turkey]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/turkey.html) to the west. Historically a zone of imperial contestation — Russian, Persian, and Ottoman — the region gained independence with the Soviet collapse and has since navigated between larger powers while managing internal conflicts. The Nagorno-Karabakh conflict between Azerbaijan and Armenia dominated regional politics for three decades until Azerbaijan's 2023 victory decisively shifted the balance [1].

The region's strategic value derives from its position as a transit corridor connecting the [Caspian Sea]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/caspian.html) basin to Turkey and Europe, and from its energy infrastructure — Azerbaijani offshore oil and gas fields that feed pipelines through Georgia and Turkey. The Middle Corridor, a China-to-Europe trade route bypassing Russia, traverses the South Caucasus, making it a node in the evolving trans-Caspian connectivity architecture [1][2].

# Key Dynamics

- **Three-power competition structures every decision.** Russia, Iran, and Turkey each project influence into the South Caucasus; no regional state can align with one without provoking reactions from the others. [1]
- **Karabakh resolution shifted the balance.** Azerbaijan's 2023 victory defeated Iran-aligned Armenia, reducing Tehran's influence along its northern border and consolidating the Turkey-Azerbaijan axis. [1][2]
- **The Trump Route creates a new geopolitical corridor.** The US-mediated Armenia-Azerbaijan agreement connecting Azerbaijan to Nakhchivan along the Iranian border opens a transit route to Turkey and Europe, constraining Iran's northern reach. [1]
- **Israel's diplomatic ring encircles Iran from the north.** Israel's deep partnership with Azerbaijan and its first permanent embassy in Ashgabat (Turkmenistan, ~20km from the Iranian border) form a northern arc of engagement around Iran. [2]
- **Russia's southern grip is eroding.** The Ukraine war has degraded Russia's capacity to project power into the South Caucasus; Friedman calls the region "in some ways the soft underbelly of Russia." [1]
- **Energy infrastructure is the economic anchor.** Caspian offshore fields and the pipeline network through Georgia and Turkey give the region strategic value beyond its population or territory. [1]

# Analysis

## The Three-Power Competition

The South Caucasus is a classic shatter belt where three major powers — Russia, Iran, and Turkey — compete for influence. Russia's position has weakened as the Ukraine war consumes its military resources and political attention. Iran's influence was dealt a blow by Azerbaijan's 2023 Karabakh victory, which defeated Iran-aligned Armenia and reduced Tehran's leverage along its northern border. Turkey, backing Azerbaijan, emerged as the ascendant external power. The US, [China]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/countries/china.html), and the [EU]({{ site.baseurl }}/actors/organizations/eu.html) all maintain active engagement, adding further layers to an already complex strategic environment [1][2].

## The Trump Route and Regional Reconfiguration

The Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity (TRIPP) — a US-mediated Armenia-Azerbaijan agreement connecting Azerbaijan to its Nakhchivan exclave along the Iranian border — is the most significant geopolitical reconfiguration in the region since the Karabakh resolution. The corridor creates a physical link from the Caspian through Azerbaijan to Turkey and Europe, bypassing both Russia and Iran. For Azerbaijan, it transforms the country from a terminal energy node into a transit hub. For Iran, it represents a potential constraint on its northern reach. For Armenia, it represents a forced normalization with the adversary that defeated it. The route's implementation will depend on sustained US engagement and the willingness of all parties to accept its geopolitical implications [1].

## Israel's Diplomatic Ring

Israel's strategic presence in the South Caucasus and adjacent Caspian states forms a northern arc around Iran. The deep Israel-Azerbaijan partnership, dating from independence, provides Israel with a perch on Iran's northern border and a base for intelligence operations. Israel's opening of its first permanent embassy in Ashgabat, Turkmenistan, in April 2023 — approximately 20km from the Iranian border — extended this ring eastward. Together, these positions give Israel a contiguous presence along Iran's northern frontier, from the South Caucasus to [Central Asia]({{ site.baseurl }}/regions/central-asia.html) [2].
